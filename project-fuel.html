Project Overview

Problem · Approach · Result

The Problem

A retail fuel station operating 6 pumps and serving 5 customer segments — motorcycles, cars, buses, trucks, and generators — needed to understand where its revenue was coming from and whether it was running efficiently.

Management had raw transaction data across 12 months but no answers to: Which fuel type drives the most revenue? Which customers are most valuable? Are all 6 pumps working properly? Is the station actually profitable?

Without answers, operational decisions were being made by habit — not data.

How I Solved It

The dataset came as 12 separate monthly Excel sheets. Using Power Query, I appended all 12 sheets into one consolidated dataset of 31,896 records, standardised column names and data types, checked for duplicates and missing values (none were found), and created calculated columns for total revenue, profit margins, and time-based metrics.

I then built an interactive Excel dashboard covering revenue by fuel type, customer segment, pump, attendant, and time period — including hourly, daily, and monthly breakdowns. This gave management a single view of the full year's performance for the first time.

What I Found & Recommended

Trucks are the most valuable customers — generating 56.98% of total revenue despite being one of five segments. The station's commercial health depends on truck service quality.

Pump 4 is a red flag — consistently recording the lowest revenue across all 6 pumps throughout the year. Whether mechanical, operational, or attendance-related, this needs investigation.

December and 3–5 PM are the highest-demand periods, but neither is being planned for in staffing or stock. The station has a normal 3.68% margin — but at that level, every avoidable inefficiency hurts.

Deep Dive

Key Insights

Each insight below shows exactly what I found in the data, why it matters to the business, and what I recommended to fix it.

Insight 01
Trucks generate 56.98% of total revenue — they are the station's most critical customer by far
Despite serving five different vehicle segments, trucks dominate revenue so heavily that the station's financial health depends almost entirely on them. PMS (petrol) accounts for 59.04% of revenue and AGO (diesel, primarily used by trucks) for 37.46% — these two fuels together make up 96.5% of total revenue. This means the station's product strategy is essentially already decided by the data: everything else is secondary.
→ Dedicate faster refuelling lanes for trucks during peak hours. Ensure AGO is always stocked. Consider a truck-driver loyalty programme to lock in repeat commercial volume — this is the single highest-value relationship the station has.
Insight 02
Pump 4 is the lowest-revenue pump in the station — and the cause is unknown
Pump 4 recorded the lowest revenue across all 6 pumps consistently throughout the year. In a station where pumps should theoretically share similar traffic, one that persistently underperforms signals a problem — but the data alone cannot tell us whether it is a mechanical/accuracy issue, an attendant performance issue, or a customer avoidance pattern (e.g. awkward positioning or longer wait times). The pattern is clear; the cause needs investigation.
→ Inspect Pump 4 for mechanical accuracy issues. Review attendant assignment patterns at that pump. Compare transaction count (not just revenue) to diagnose whether it is a volume problem or a per-transaction value problem — the fix depends on the answer.
Insight 03
December and 3–5 PM are the highest-demand periods — but neither is being planned for
December recorded the highest monthly revenue across the full year, driven by festive travel and increased transportation demand. Within each day, the 3–5 PM window is the peak transaction period — this aligns with closing-hour logistics and truck route schedules. DPK (kerosene) also showed a sales spike between March and May, suggesting a seasonal household fuel demand pattern. None of these patterns are currently reflected in staffing levels or stock ordering cycles.
→ Pre-stock additional PMS and AGO before December. Assign maximum attendant staffing during the 3–5 PM window every day. Adjust DPK ordering to anticipate the March–May demand uptick rather than reacting to it after shelves run low.
Insight 04
A 3.68% gross margin is industry-normal — but at this level, small inefficiencies have a big impact
A 3.68% gross margin is within the normal range for retail fuel stations where commodity pricing compresses margins. But at this margin level, small operational leaks — a low-performing pump, overstocking the wrong fuel, or under-staffing the peak window — have a disproportionate effect on profitability. The station has very little room for waste. Every pump, every attendant, and every stock decision either protects the margin or erodes it.
→ Expand high-margin ancillary products: engine oil, car accessories, convenience store items. These require minimal operational change but directly improve the margin without needing to increase fuel volume. Monitor pump accuracy regularly to prevent undetected fuel loss.

Recommendations

These are the specific actions I recommended based on the data findings.

🚛 Maximise Truck Revenue
Add dedicated faster-refuelling lanes for trucks during peak hours. Introduce a loyalty scheme for regular truck drivers. Ensure AGO is always adequately stocked as the primary fuel for this revenue segment.
🔧 Investigate Pump 4
Inspect for mechanical or accuracy issues immediately. Review whether it is assigned less experienced attendants. Compare transaction count vs revenue to isolate whether this is a volume problem or a per-transaction value problem.
📦 Plan for Peak Periods
Pre-stock for December demand surge. Increase DPK orders in anticipation of the March–May seasonal uptick. Assign maximum staff coverage every day during the 3–5 PM peak window to prevent lost sales from queue build-up.
💡 Expand Revenue Streams
Add high-margin ancillary products — engine oil, car accessories, and convenience store items. These require minimal operational change but directly improve the 3.68% gross margin without needing to increase fuel volume.
📊 Monitor Attendant Performance
Review attendant sales distribution and workload regularly. Identify consistently low-performing attendant assignments and investigate whether this is a training, scheduling, or placement issue.
🛢️ DPK Strategy
Monitor the March–May seasonal pattern for one more year before making major DPK inventory decisions. Apply targeted promotions only if consistent demand is confirmed; otherwise prioritise PMS and AGO stock.
